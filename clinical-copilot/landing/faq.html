Product

What does the Co-Pilot actually do?

Three checkers, two of them live today:

  • Diagnostic Checker (live): reads the chart, returns a ranked list of candidate diagnoses, each backed by a citation into the patient's own FHIR resources. If it cannot cite, it does not claim.
  • Chart Error Checker (live, Pro tier): flags internal contradictions, like a med recorded as active that the latest diagnosis no longer supports, units that disagree across encounters, or an allergy buried three encounters back that contraindicates an active order.
  • Contraindication Checker (coming soon, Pro tier): cross-references medications against allergies, conditions, and each other for known interactions.

The clinician decides what to do with the output. The Co-Pilot does not prescribe, order, or modify the chart.

Why is it different from other AI diagnostic tools?

Two things matter more than any single technique. First, every claim ships with a citation into the patient's own chart. The engine refuses to answer a question it cannot back up from a FHIR resource. Second, every prompt, every answer, and every citation lands in a hash-chained audit log that an auditor can replay without re-running the LLM. Together those make Clinical Co-Pilot defensible during a post-incident review in a way most clinical AI tools are not.

What patient data does the sidecar read?

The standard FHIR resource set: Patient, Condition, MedicationRequest, AllergyIntolerance, Observation, Encounter, Procedure, DocumentReference. All read-only. The sidecar holds an OAuth access token scoped exclusively to these read endpoints; it cannot write anything.

Will the Co-Pilot ever write to the chart?

Document ingest (Pro tier) is the only flow that produces structured FHIR write-back, and every suggested resource is shown to the clinician for confirmation before it lands. The Diagnostic Checker and Chart Error Checker are strictly read-only. There is no automatic charting.

Compliance and security

Is the Co-Pilot HIPAA compliant?

HIPAA does not have a certification you can hand someone. What we can say: the architecture is aligned with the HIPAA Security Rule and the OCR audit protocol. We sign a Business Associate Agreement with every paying customer. OpenAI is on our BAA chain via their Enterprise tier with Zero Data Retention enabled. The audit log meets the seven-year retention requirement.

Full posture: trust.html.

Do you sign a BAA?

Yes. Our customer-facing BAA template is at baa.html. Enterprise tier customers can swap in their own BAA after counsel redlines ours.

Does OpenAI train on the prompts we send?

No. We use OpenAI on the Enterprise tier with Zero Data Retention enabled. OpenAI does not retain prompts or completions, and does not use the data to train models. Our contract with them is the standard Enterprise BAA.

What about the reranker? Does PHI go to Cohere?

No. The rerank step runs on de-identified candidate strings produced by our internal de-identifier (HIPAA Safe Harbor identifiers removed). The mapping back to the original is held in memory inside our sidecar; Cohere never sees PHI. That is why a BAA with Cohere is not necessary by design.

How does the audit log work?

Every clinician prompt, every AI response, every citation, and the exact set of FHIR resources fetched is appended to a hash-chained log. Each row hashes the previous row's hash plus the new payload, so tampering is detectable. Daily the chain head is written to AWS S3 Object Lock with a seven-year retention policy. An auditor can replay any session from the log without re-running the LLM.

What about SOC 2?

SOC 2 Type II is in progress on Vanta. The observation window is six months; the Type II report is expected within twelve months of the first paying hospital customer. The Type I report can be made available under NDA earlier on request. Pre-SOC, we publish a pre-filled CAIQ and VSAQ at VENDOR_SECURITY_QUESTIONNAIRE.md.

HITRUST?

Available for Enterprise tier customers who make it a contract condition. Cost runs $60K to $100K for the formal assessment; we will not pursue it until a hospital contract justifies the spend.

Install and operations

What OpenEMR versions are supported?

OpenEMR 7.0 and newer, PHP 8.2 or newer. The module is packaged in the OpenEMR custom module convention and registers via the standard event-listener system; no core patch is required.

Can I self-host the sidecar?

Yes. Multi-arch Docker images are published on GitHub Container Registry on every release tag. The full stack runs from clinical-copilot/deploy/docker-compose.openemr-sidecar.yml. Enterprise tier includes quarterly upgrade ride-along.

How long is install?

For most clinics: five minutes from zip download to a working button on the patient summary. Step-by-step at install.html.

What if my OpenEMR is behind a corporate proxy?

The module's outbound traffic is only to the sidecar URL you configure. If you self-host, that can be a private address inside your VPC and never crosses the public internet. If you use the managed sidecar, allowlist api.copilot.scott-lydon.dev on the proxy.
